## Job Market Paper

**Agency Conflicts and Financial Covenants:
	Theory and Estimates of Control Rights**<br>
October 2026. <!-- [Paper](/papers/paper1.pdf). -->

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="abstract-content">
This paper studies how agency conflicts shape the design and enforcement of financial covenants in private debt markets. Using a panel of U.S. syndicated loans from 1996 to 2020, I document a substantial evolution in covenant use and enforcement: the annual incidence of covenant violations declines from 20 percent to below 5 percent over two decades, while the creditor intervention rate following a violation rises from 40 percent to over 70 percent. Importantly, this change is accompanied by a shift in the channels of creditor control rights: while covenant violations continue to constrain investment, their effects on operating income and managerial discipline weaken considerably. To quantify these forces, I develop a micro-founded optimal contracting model in which covenant responds to managerial moral hazard, performance signals, and noncontractible interim actions. By allocating contingent control rights, financial covenants mitigate the lack of cash-flow pledgeability and facilitate financing. I structurally estimate the model using the Simulated Method of Moments, targeting causally identified moments from the reduced-form literature. The estimates show a substantial decrease in both the performance benefits and the managerial costs of creditor intervention. Private-equity sponsorship explains roughly one quarter of this shift, pointing to a broader evolution in the nature of creditor governance.
</div>
</details>

## Working Papers

**The Loan Renegotiation Channel of Quantitative Easing**<br>
with Lin Xie (Minnesota Carlson). October 2026. [Paper](/papers/qe_renegotiation.pdf), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7622438).

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="abstract-content">
We study a channel of quantitative easing (QE) that operates through the renegotiation of existing bank loans. Matching syndicated-loan amendment histories to bank balance sheets, we find that larger purchases of agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) are associated with more renegotiation at exposed banks, with the increase concentrated in amendments that favor lenders. Relative to other loans of the same firm in the same quarter, a doubling of quarterly purchases is associated with committed amounts 2.5 percent lower, spreads 10.6 basis points higher, and maturities 3.1 percent shorter on exposed banks’ amendments. The response is present among borrowers in covenant compliance and no stronger among those in violation, distinguishing it from renegotiation driven by creditor control rights in technical default. It is concentrated among well-capitalized, well-provisioned, and profitable banks and among less profitable, lower-valuation, but not highly leveraged borrowers, consistent with a bargaining channel in which financially strong lenders extract larger concessions from borrowers with weaker outside options. A shift-share design indicates that a ten-percentage-point increase in lender-favorable renegotiation lowers firms’ annual investment rate by 6 percent of its mean. QE thus tightened existing corporate credit for borrowers of exposed banks, potentially offsetting part of its expansionary effects on new lending.
</div>
</details>

**Endogenously Imperfect Risk Sharing and Financial Amplification**<br>
October 2026. [Paper](/papers/riskamplification.pdf). 

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="abstract-content">
Why do financial contracts leave entrepreneurs exposed to aggregate risk even when contracts can be made fully state-contingent? I develop a financial accelerator model with a moral hazard problem in which private benefit of low effort is non-monetary and incentive compatibility holds state by state. 
Since the marginal value of entrepreneurial net worth is countercyclical, the incentive constraint tightens procyclically. Entrepreneurs thus take advantage of this tradeoff by offloading more idiosyncratic risk at the cost of taking on more aggregate risk.
Imperfect aggregate risk sharing therefore emerges endogenously from the interaction between state-dependent incentives and risk sharing, rather than from contractual restrictions. This exposure amplifies aggregate shocks: entrepreneurial net worth becomes more sensitive to macroeconomic conditions, compressing borrowing capacity procyclically. Quantitatively, state-contingent contracting does not eliminate the financial accelerator, and the model generates substantial and persistent amplification of investment and output.
</div>
</details>
